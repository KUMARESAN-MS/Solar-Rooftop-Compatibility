Here's the pitch.

Imagine you're wondering whether it's worth putting solar panels on your roof, but you have no idea how to figure that out — you don't know your area's sunlight levels, what size system you'd need, or whether it'll actually save you money. That's the problem this app solves. You open it, drop a pin on your house on a map, and answer a few things anyone would know off the top of their head: what kind of building it is, roughly how big your rooftop is (and how much of it is actually clear versus cluttered with water tanks or stairs), and your monthly electricity bill or units consumed. That's it — no technical inputs, no sunlight hours, no jargon.

Behind the scenes, the system takes your location and pulls real solar and weather data for that spot — how much sunlight your area gets on average, across the year — from a free public solar database. It combines that with the roof info and consumption info you gave it, and runs it through a physics-based calculation (the same kind of math real solar companies use) to estimate how much electricity a solar system at your location could realistically generate, accounting for things like heat reducing panel efficiency and normal system losses.

This is where the AI/ML piece comes in: rather than trusting the raw physics formula blindly, the system also runs your inputs through a machine learning model that's been trained to fine-tune that estimate — essentially learning patterns in how real-world solar output tends to differ from the simple textbook calculation, and correcting for that. So the ML isn't decoration; it's specifically the part that makes the generation estimate smarter and more realistic than a plain formula alone.

From there, the system looks at two things — how big a system your roof can physically fit, and how big a system would actually offset your electricity usage — and recommends the sweet spot between the two, rather than just throwing 3kW/5kW/7kW options at you and letting you guess. It also shows you a small comparison table so you can see the trade-offs.

Finally, you get a clean results screen: your recommended system size, expected yearly electricity generation, estimated installation cost, expected annual savings, roughly how many years until the system pays for itself, and how much CO2 you'd be avoiding each year — all in one summary, with the option to dig into more detail (monthly generation, size comparisons, assumptions used) if you want it. You can save your property so next time you open the app, it's already there, and you can tweak any input and instantly see updated results.
